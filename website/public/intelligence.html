Find jobs worth your time

A shortlist ranked against your experience and preferences.

Job boards of your country

Names the boards people really use for your kind of work.

Data usedcountries, places, role wordsNever: your CVHow it works

Your AI names the job boards people really use in your countries for your kind of work (a general board, a developer or hospitality board), with their public search page; Jobadabra reads those pages with your own search words, like any careers page. A board that refuses automated reading is offered for you to open; one that never has jobs is dropped. Your AI sees your countries, places and role words, never your CV.

A scout that keeps learning

Looks for employers and career pages, and learns from each probe.

Data usedplace words, page text of a careers pageNever: your CVHow it works

Your AI proposes employers, their own job sites and company lists to look at, and with Claude Code searches the web for a job site no address leads to, then learns from what each probe found. It starts from the companies of the countries your places are in (your AI says which country each place word is in), and your AI says, in any language, whether a careers page has open jobs, none right now, or is no careers page at all; that answer is kept for the page. The shared employer list grows with every install.

Reads any job site

Works out a job list it has never seen, once, then remembers it.

Data usedan outline of the pageNever: anything about youHow it works

When the extension's quick guess cannot read a job list you opened, your AI reads an outline of the page (its repeated blocks and paging buttons, a few samples, never anything about you) and says which blocks are jobs, which line is the title, employer and place, and how to reach the next page. That recipe is kept for the site, so the next visit needs no AI; your AI is asked again only when the site changes. A fast model reads first; when it finds no list, a stronger one tries that page once.

Roles that fit you

Suggests neighbouring roles: a photographer’s studio assistant, a seller’s cashier.

Data usedyour Profile, search wordsNever: the jobsHow it works

On Strategy, your AI reads your Profile and your role words and suggests up to eight neighbouring roles you could do (a photographer: studio assistant, retoucher; a shop seller: cashier, visual merchandiser), each with the word to search for and how many open jobs it would add in your places. One click adds it; "Not now" sets them aside for good. At most once a day; your AI sees your Profile and search words, never the jobs.

Knows where each job is

Says if a job is in your places and whether you may work there.

Data usedlocation names, your places, work rightsNever: your CVHow it works

Your places are read by your AI, not matched as words: each new job location ("1211 Genf", "Biel/Bienne", "Wallisellen", "Remote - EMEA") is shown to your AI with your places as you wrote them (best places, anywhere in, abroad, remote or not), and it says whether the job is in one of your best places, in your places, or outside them, and whether your work rights as you wrote them (a permit, a citizenship, "EU") let you work there without a visa. When a location names no place of its own (only "Switzerland", or nothing), your AI reads the start of that posting to find where the job is ("at the Geneva site"), on any site; until then the job is neither scored nor closed. Each location and each such posting is decided once; changing your places or work rights asks again. Your AI sees location names, your place words and your work rights, and for those postings their title and text, never your CV.

Sorts titles your words miss

A “Client Advisor” can be a shop seller: it judges, in any language.

Data usedjob titles, your search wordsNever: anything elseHow it works

Only the place is strict. A job in your places whose title your role words miss ("Client Advisor", "Collaborateur Magasin" for a shop seller) is shown to your AI with your search words; it says which titles could be your kind of work, in any language. Each title is decided once, then the fit score grades the job as usual. Your AI sees only titles and your search words.

An answer for every empty field

Proposes what each empty box needs, from your details or CV.

Data usedthe field’s label, your detailsNever: consents, demographics, facts only you knowHow it works

When a form is left with empty fields, the Applying page shows each one with its proposed answer: what the fill tried and the form did not take, or the detail it asks for, from your details or your CV. Your AI reads which detail a label asks for ("Rue et numéro" is your street, "Formule d'appel" your salutation), in any language, from the label alone, once per label. For a menu, your AI picks the form's own choice that means the same ("Monsieur" on a Madam/Sir menu is Sir), never a word the form doesn't offer. Use fills it in the form and keeps it for future forms; you can change it first. When nothing you gave states it, your AI types your most likely answer, when it is fairly sure, from your Profile and what the posting expects (a salary, years with a tool, a yes/no screening question), and you check every answer before you submit. A low-confidence guess, or a guess at a contact detail, a link, a knockout question (work permit, sponsorship, location) or a choice such as SMS notifications is only proposed, for you to confirm; never anything for consents, demographic questions or facts only you know.

The job list, not the home page

Looks for a site’s real list of jobs before reading it.

Data usedthe site’s linksHow it works

Before a site you can only open yourself is read, Jobadabra looks for its job list: the site's own Careers link first, then your AI's pick among its links, then a "<company> jobs" web search, preferring your country's page. The address is kept for the site; one that shows no jobs twice is dropped and looked for again.

Gets past a dead end

Picks up to two steps toward the list: a “See all jobs” button, a country picker.

Data usedfirst lines of the page, your search wordsNever: your CV; it never signs in, applies or paysHow it works

When a job site you opened shows no jobs even after your AI's reading (a "See all jobs" button, a country picker, a search box), your AI picks at most two steps toward the list among the page's own buttons, links and search boxes, from its first lines of text and your search words, never your CV. It never signs in, applies, saves, pays or answers a robot check: when the page needs you for that, it says so and stops. When the fast model sees no way on, a stronger one looks once more. Cookie banners are closed with the least consent offered.

Filters for your search

On LinkedIn, Indeed or an employer’s site, picks the page’s filters that fit you.

Data usedfilter names, your search wordsNever: your CV, the pageHow it works

On a job list you opened yourself (LinkedIn, Indeed, an employer's site), the extension's "Filter for my search, then read" asks your AI which of that page's own filters fit your search: your places, your kind of role, recent jobs. Your AI sees only the filters' names and your search words, never your CV or the page. It never touches Apply, Sign in, Save or Message; then the jobs on the page are read and scored like any other.

Your details from your CV

Fills in phone, street, town and more from your CV; you check and Save.

Data usedyour CV, your ProfileNever: nothing is kept until you SaveHow it works

The details forms ask for and you haven't given yet (phone, street, postal code, town, date of birth, salutation) are read from your CV by your AI (and from your Profile, for what only it says, such as your town), once per CV and Profile, and put in the empty boxes of Profile → Your details, each marked "From your CV". Nothing is kept until you check them and press Save; a detail you typed is never replaced. Your AI never guesses: a salutation is proposed only when your CV shows it, and it and anything taken from your Profile are marked "check it".

Postings with no form

Finds the email, call or visit that says how to apply.

Data used12 short lines of the postingNever: what you typed, your dataHow it works

When the page does not say clearly how to apply, the AI reads up to 12 short numbered lines of the posting (sentences, links, buttons, an address or a phone number; never what you typed, never your data) and names the one that says how to apply: an email, a call, a visit. It can only point at a line that is on the page; it never sends or presses anything for you.

Understands any language

Interview or rejection? It decides from a fixed list, in any language.

Data usedone item at a timeNever: your mail, in the shared listHow it works

Whether a calendar event is an interview, a reply asks you to book a time, a round was a recruiter screen or a technical one, which code and link in a sign-up email confirm your account: your AI decides from a fixed list of answers, in any language, once per item, instead of English keyword lists. Without AI, the answers you got before still come, from a shared list of meanings that starts with the old rules and grows with every language an install learns (only public wording: places, titles, labels; never your mail).

Gmail and Calendar, read-only

Each email matched to the right application.

Data usedsender, subject, first lineNever: unrelated mail in fullHow it works

Each email sorted into replies, interviews, rejections or recruiter pitches, and matched to the right application. Any language: no keyword lists. Only those are read in full, and each email is judged once.
